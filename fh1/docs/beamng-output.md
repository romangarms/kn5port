# BeamNG output: rendering, terrain, LODs

How FH1 data becomes a level. Constants are at the top of `build3.py`.

## Terrain and ground

**Ground is chosen by FH1's zone hexes** (`HEXGROUND`, the default; `FH1_HEXGROUND=0` goes back to the far tiers below). Each 100 m hex lists what FH1 draws while the player is in it, and each zone record gives its LOD group's switch distances. `hex_selection` gives each 16 m cell the groups that the nearest hex listing each group draws at that distance; cells no hex draws take the finest ground over them. `hex_split` keeps a triangle up to two cells across whole where any cell it covers is chosen, so groups overlap by a triangle where they hand over rather than leaving slivers, and splits bigger ones only where the selection changes under them. Cutting on the cell lines left T-junction cracks. Far tiers, coverage clipping and water and rock rules don't touch ground in this mode, and `TERR_CUBE` is never drawn: as a fill it put Carson's town-block boxes through the houses, and height rules for water and rock dropped ground along walls and at waterlines. The user checked Clear Springs on 2026-11-04: no visible holes. Offline on Clear Springs, no cell within reach of a hex lacks ground and 1.3% of cells hold two groups within 1 m of height, nearly all ground against a river's surface mesh.

FH1's own terrain meshes are the visible ground and the collision. The level has no `TerrainBlock`: the user tested hiding it and asked for it to go. The heightfield notes below are history.

Far ground near a road is kept and capped just under road height, not cut away. `TERR_CUBE` stays out: enabling it added almost no coverage. Sections listing a red and green blend mask as a colour layer use a real layer instead (`rgmask`), or they draw yellow.

`TERR` decides what is ground. Mountains Area 3, around Beaumont, is named `TERR_Moun_A3_LOD00_04` and so on, and once missed it: it drew as props with one texture and wasn't counted as coverage, so MIDDIST and UberLOD ground was laid over it. The festival's fine ground, `Festival_Area5_09_LOD00` and so on, was missed the same way. `TERR_Zone*` (sparse, plain diffuse) and `TERR_Fuji_Waterfall` (additive) stay out on purpose.

All LODs of a world mesh go in the cell of the group's centre (`group_cell`), so they switch together. Splitting them by triangle left ground missing at distance. A group and its levels come from FH1's draw table (`lod_rings`, see `formats.md`), with names only as a fallback. By name alone, every `Object025` on the map was one group 12 km wide, so a cliff could lose its far LOD to an unrelated mesh's and vanish past 300 m. A group over 1 km (long cliffs, rivers, armco) has no single cell, so `cell_split` cuts its triangles along the cell lines. Split by centroid instead, Clear Springs' cliff (`Object025`) and river (`Object026`) had holes and coarse triangles poking through wherever neighbouring cells drew different details. Terrain LOD1 covers LOD2, to save memory.

Placed solid objects are instanced: one `m<mesh>.dae` shared by forest items (`fh1_m<mesh>`, `TSForestItemData`, no annotation as BeamNG has no `PROP` one), not `TSStatic`s, as Colorado had 61k of them (`PROPFOREST`; `FH1_PROPFOREST=0` keeps `TSStatic`s). Forest items collide through the shape's `Colmesh`, as trees do; the user drove Red Rock on them (2026-11-03) and found them good. Glowing props (lamp posts, for `instanceColor` and `nightEmissive`) and the shapes `animated.py` swaps out stay `TSStatic`s (`KEEPSTATIC`). Mirrored ones stay baked into cells.

- Heights come from terrain meshes rastered into tiers: LOD0, 1, 2, then far meshes. Roads are rastered separately.
- Where meshes, roads or rock are shown, the heightfield drops to `minimum_filter(H, 31) - 8 m` so it can't poke through. That window is in grid cells, so it's 62 m on Red Rock and 124 m on Colorado. Coverage holes are closed and the mask grows by 3 cells: the old erosion let the heightfield rise around every small hole.
- Origin-local meshes are left out of the world path (`origin_local`), or they appear raw at (0, 0) by the festival. Any zone record away from the origin marks a mesh, and its LOD group, as local.
- Near roads the height is capped at road height minus 0.2 m, rising with distance out to 40 m.
- Far terrain only shows where nothing better covers it. `far_tiers` orders it per triangle by how close FH1 ever draws it: the distance to the nearest zone hex listing any LOD of its mesh (see `Colorado_00.hex` in `formats.md`), in bands at 150, 400, 800 and 1500 m, then coarse (under 1,500 tris per km2) after fine. Each tier only fills what earlier tiers left. The `UberLOD` shell is never drawn within about 1 km of itself, so finer ground claims that space first. FH1 ships copies of some ground in other meshes, such as `Mountains_A1C_Terrain_05_NOLOD` repeating 4,203 triangles of `UberLOD23` and coarse LODs listed beside LOD00, and copies in one tier z-fight. Within a tier, Uber meshes go first, then finer LODs, and a triangle whose three corners (to 5 cm, `vkeys`) another mesh already drew is dropped before clipping, so the backdrop gets the same. That took exact duplicates from 12,880 triangles to 783. A height test on a raster was tried first: on steep slopes it needed up to 20 m of tolerance and dropped finer ground near Beaumont. Names misled here: `TERR_UberLOD_Patch` is the shell, and a `NOLOD` tile can be ten times finer than the shell over it. Two equally coarse meshes that FH1 draws from the same hexes, such as `NoLOD_06` and `MIDDIST_02` near Redstone, still overlap. Large placed structures (50 m and up, such as Finley Dam's `MOUN_DAM_BLDG_MainDam`) stand in holes in the terrain meshes, so `structures` rasters their footprints into `Rock` before the far tiers. Bridges, turbines, conveyors and light rigs are left out, as they span open ground. Adding `TERR_CUBE` instead was tried: it barely helped at the dam and added 280k coarse triangles over finer ground elsewhere. `UberLOD` and lake-shader meshes are exempt from the 6 km size limit. `Mountains_Water_03_NOLOD`, Finley's reservoir, shares its mesh with water 14 km off the map, and `add` drops that part: they're the ground under the eastern trees.
- `clip_far` splits triangles and keeps the parts outside coverage. Inside coverage, far vertices are first held to the near ground's height (`top`, the lowest cell in a 3-cell window, since `raster` keeps each cell's highest point), then sunk. Sinking alone left coarse mountain sides up to 17 m above the fine ground's edge near Beaumont. Far ground runs `LIP` metres under nearer ground, sloping down, so a height mismatch can't open a crack. Within 40 m of a road, far ground is capped near road height so it can't form panels across it.
- A zone record's `lod` field holds FH1's own switch distances, such as `[400, 650, 131008]`, where 131008 means never. The build doesn't read them yet. LOD1 and LOD2 files can have no record of their own, so a missing record only proves a mesh unused at LOD0.
- Terrain meshes cover 66% of the Colorado square. The rest is mostly edges and off-map land, showing the BeamNG terrain with map art as its base colour.
- FH1's far mountains aren't in its sky: `realtimesky.zip` holds only clouds, stars, moon and sun. They're world meshes up to 18 km from the centre and 2,700 m high, such as `UberLOD23`, `UberLOD24`, `Mountains_Area5_NOLOD_*` and `Mountains_A1C_Terrain_*_NOLOD`, about 10k triangles past the square. `BACKDROP` (`FH1_BACKDROP`, on for the whole map only, since a chunk would pull in the rest of the map) lifts the chunk box and the 6 km size limit. `add()` then hands triangles past the square to `backdrop()` instead of dropping them, but only LOD2-level ones. Far tiers skip `clip_far` there, as it would hold them to the edge cells' heights. `backdrop()` puts them in one shape, `c0_bakf`, with no collision and never culled. Near-path ground goes first, then far meshes one at a time, finest (shortest median edge) first, on a 16 m grid. With whole tiers at one priority, `Mountains_Area5_NOLOD` (once missed by `TERR`, so drawn as a one-texture prop) z-fought the Uber shell and `Mountains_A1C`. Coverage is eroded by a cell, so neighbouring tiles of one fineness can't eat each other's border: a coarser triangle wholly over finer ground is dropped, and its corners over finer ground sink 4 m under it. Water isn't counted as cover, so lake beds stay. `visibleDistance` is 24000 with the backdrop, so the whole map's terrain is drawn too.
- `theTerrain.ter` is format 9: u32 size, heights, layer map, one material.

## Terrain blending (user confirmed "perfect")

`h_blnd*` shaders blend slots 0, 1 and 5 using the mask in slot 2, read with UV set 2 as `(s + 1) / 2`.

BeamNG equivalent: `activeLayers` 3, with layers 1 and 2 using `opacityMap` and `opacityMapUseUV: 1`. See `blend_material`. Layers tile UV0 at FH1's per-material scales (`blend_layers`: `c360_0.xy`, `.zw`, `c360_1.xy`), mostly 4x or 6x: before 2026-11-04 they were drawn at 1x, four to six times too coarse. UV0 goes out at layer A's scale, and other layers draw from detail maps (`layer_stage`).

## Surfaces

`h_road*` shaders are roads. `role_ground` sets asphalt or dirt from section names. The user confirmed dirt roads work. Rock uses `ROCK`, barriers and rails `METAL`.

Road and `h_vblnd` blending follows FH1's decoded shaders (formats.md, `.fxobj` shaders), in `layered` and `skirt_passes`. Each layer tiles at its material's own scale. BeamNG has no per-layer UV scale, so UV0 goes out at layer A's scale (or the noise's), and a layer at another scale takes its colour from a detail map at its own `detailScale`: `half_color` stores half the linear colour, as `saturate(2d)` at strength 1 gives it back. Vertex weights go in UV set 1 through 64 px curve textures (`fh1_c_*`, BC5 for 8-bit precision), interpolated across each triangle as FH1's are. FH1 adds weight and noise before clamping, and BeamNG can only multiply its two opacity textures, so `blend_fit` finds the nearest product per material (slopes and noise texture), noise remapped into `fh1_q*`: rms error 0.11 to 0.28 against FH1's curve, where the hand-set ramp it replaced was 0.19 to 0.32. Noise uses red for layer B and green for C, at its real tiling. FH1's darkening (w squared times AO) is the vertex colour (`vertColor`), per vertex: averaged per section, as `road_tone` did before 2026-11-04, it stepped sharply at section edges, square patches across Route 29. `*_modulate` adds a black layer at opacity z (1 - map) where a UV1 axis is free; the map is read on UV0, as FH1's TEXCOORD1 isn't decoded for roads. Skirts are verges over the terrain, opaque at the road's edge and fading out by `1 - byte 3`; they were drawn the other way round as an opaque blend into the road before. Each of their layers is its own translucent pass, as a translucent BeamNG material mixes layer weights into its alpha.

Decals (`h_vblnd_decal*`, 310k triangles of lines, tyre marks, tar seams and pothole patches) are translucent, receive shadows and cast none. Their opacity is the texture's alpha times the vertex alpha, which goes in UV set 1 through a half-height ramp used as the opacity detail map, so the product is exact. `decal_mask` sections keep their mask on UV set 1 instead, so their vertex alpha becomes one `opacityFactor`, the section mean rounded to 0.05. Before this, opaque DXT1 patches such as `0x321B` showed as solid squares and tyre marks were about six times too dark. `h_road` sections on meshes named `Decal`, such as drains, are treated as decals so they stop z-fighting the road.

Textures are written as pre-cooked DXT1 `.dds` files by `dds.py`, capped at `TEXMAX = 1024`. A DXT1 top mip keeps FH1's own blocks unchanged. Lower mips, masks and DXT3/5 sources are encoded in `dds.py`. Colorado's PNGs were cooked up to 1024 px by BeamNG for an unknown reason (3.56 GB against 213 MB now), which caused the out-of-VRAM load. Red Rock's PNGs weren't. Keep the `.color.` and `.data.` suffixes: BeamNG picks the colour space from them.

The user has said terrain looks a little low-res. FH1's sources are mostly 128 to 512 px and only 22 reach `TEXMAX`, so raising it won't help.

Normal maps are `n<id>.normal.dds`, BC5U like stock maps, keeping FH1's top-mip blocks. `section_normals` sets `NM`, the current section's colour-to-normal pairs, which `material` and `blend_material` read, as `LV` and `FIX` work. Each normal goes on the stage drawing its colour layer, chosen by gradient correlation, falling back to the first layer. Material names gain `_n<id>`.

## Water

Lake shader sections go in `wat` shapes: no collision, never culled, with FH1's ripple normals on the translucent material. `water.py` (run by `build3.py` after `particles.py`) rasters them at 3 m, traces each piece of water's centre line, the longest path through it weighted to keep away from the banks, then branches the first ribbon misses. Each becomes a `River` in `fh1_water`: nodes every 40 m or less, at FH1's surface height, with width from the distance to the bank plus 4 m and depth to the lowest ground under the water, so bridges don't count. The `wat` TSStatics are removed, and saved in `fh1_water/meshwater.json` so a rerun can read them. Ripples are FH1's two lake normals as RGB DXT1 (`w<id>.normal.dds`), reflections come from `fh1_sky_cubemap`, and the depth ramp and foam are BeamNG's core ones, with foam off.

## Particles

BeamNG 0.40 crashes on level load (integer divide by zero, `BeamNG.drive.x64.exe` + 0x9def55) in two particle cases. With `animateTexture`, `preload` reads `animTexTiling` as 0 0, although `"4 4"` passes `onAdd`, and the frame list divides by it. No stock content animates particle textures, so `particles.py` cuts each tile into its own particle. An emitter's `particles` list over 255 characters is dropped at load ("particle string too long"), which silently removed birds and campfire flames, so tiles are capped at 16 (`MAX_TILES`) under short `fh1q<crc>_<i>` names, and the script refuses longer lists. Separately, `preload` divides by `ejectionPeriodMS - periodVarianceMS`, and the validator clamps both to 2047 without keeping the variance below the period.

## Night lights

**Night, after live tests at Carson 22:00 (2026-11-04):** the overlays are off (`OVERLAYS`, `FH1_NIGHT_OVERLAYS=1` brings them back). They put a second copy of the light the SpotLights already cast on the same ground, washed streets to flat white and pulled the auto-exposure down so facades went black; from above the lamps alone carry the warm street glow. Lamps run at `LAMP_GAIN` 1.0 with `RANGE` 67.5-105 m, coloured from each pool's falloff (10-50% of its peak), as FH1's pool cores are near white. ScatterSky's `nightColor`, `moonLightColor`, `moonEnabled` and the stock night gradients made no visible difference to buildings or ground in the same tests, only to the sky; `sky.py` sets the first two to FH1's night hues for that. The user found the result "pretty great". The rest of this paragraph describes the overlays as built before. `nightlights.py` (run by `build3.py` after `water.py`) writes the lightmap overlays (`fh1_night`, glowing through stock `nightEmissive`, hidden by day by `fh1Extras.lua`) and lamps (`fh1_lamps`). Lamps are built like West Coast's street lamps (`lightemitters_pl_double`, `_single`, `_lightcity`): a `SpotLight` at the head pointing straight down (`rotationMatrix` [1,0,0, 0,0,-1, 0,1,0], the one they all use), `innerAngle` 25, `outerAngle` 150 to 160 and `range` 45 to 70 m growing with head height, `intensityUnit` lm with `brightness` = intensity / 5000, `isEnabled` false and `nightLight` '1', so stock `core_environment` switches every lamp from 18:00 to 6:00 and the clustered renderer culls them and budgets shadows. Brightness matches the PointLights the user approved (12000 / 20000 pi, median 0.163), scaled by the pool's peak. No lamp casts shadows (`LAMP_SHADOWS`): with shadows on the 987 lamp-post lamps, lights faded in and straight back out in-game, as BeamNG shadows only about 64 lights within a time budget. Before that they were PointLights, which `fh1Extras.lua` switched nearest-first: 20 within 250 m showed about 6, and even 250 within 1 km with a 40 to 60 m radius only showed right by the camera. Lowering `ClusterShading.minLightScreenSize` was tried and reverted, as it changes the player's settings. Lamp glass glows at night from the next rebuild: FH1's `h_diff_spec_ao_2__emissive_lm` lamps keep an emissive mask in slot position 3, black except the glass, which `build3.py` (`GLOW`) makes the material's `emissiveMap` under `instanceEmissive` (`GLOW_NITS` 3000, by eye from West Coast's floodlight at 4000), and their TSStatics carry `nightEmissive` with a black `instanceColor`.

Object lightmaps and AO maps come from `build3.py` itself, on UV1 (TEXCOORD1 through the UV box, see `formats.md`), at the slots each shader samples them from (`SAMP`, read with `xenos.py`). An AO map is the material's `ambientOcclusionMap` with `ambientOcclusionMapUseUV: 1`. FH1 applies it to ambient light only; BeamNG's AO also darkens other light, so it may look stronger. A lit lightmap (over 0.2% of texels above 40 of 255, `LM_LIT`) is an `emissiveMap` on UV1 under `instanceEmissive`, tinted by the texture's mean colour, as FH1 multiplies the lightmap by the surface colour. `LM_NITS` is 80. A model whose material holds its own lit lightmap stays a TSStatic, like the lamp glass (`GLOWING`). A placement whose lightmap comes from its draw (`s[1]`, slot `0xFFFFFFFF`) is baked into a `c<cell>_lit` cell with that material: TSStatics with `nightEmissive`, lit by stock core_environment from 18:00 to 6:00. World-mesh props with a lit lightmap go in `lit` cells too. Left out: lightmaps that only exist as the bundles' 16 px stand-ins, and LOD1 records' lightmaps, which are laid out for LOD1. `emissiveMapUseUV` works (seen in-game 2026-11-04). **Windows:** FH1's `*_emissive` building shaders light a window mask in a warm (1, 0.5, 0.32) at night: the specular map's green over half, (g - 0.5) x 2, for `h_diff_spec_refl_emissive_ao_4*` (glass with curtains drawn in), else the emissive map at slot 3. It's an `emissiveMap` on UV0 at `WIN_NITS` 350 under `instanceEmissive`, and models with windows stay TSStatics or go in `lit` cells. A section with both a lightmap and windows gets one 512 px UV1 texture, `b<lightmap>_<mask>_<hash>` (`bake_emissive`): the lightmap times the texture's mean colour, plus the mask drawn into the section's UV1 charts at each texel's UV0, both as shares of `BAKE_NITS`. `LM_NITS` is 120. **Roads:** FH1's road shaders darken the colour per vertex. `road_tone` averages the colour vertex's byte 0 squared and 0.05 + 0.95 AO (the AO map read at TEXCOORD2, bytes 24-27, the same UV box; on roads it also dims the sun) into `baseColorFactor`, rounded to 0.05, on road, road-layer and skirt-blend materials. The modulate map is drawn per pixel instead of averaged: `road_modulate` adds a last black `road_material` layer at opacity mean(byte 3) x (1 - modulate), on UV0 (`q<id>.data.dds`), which multiplies what's under it by lerp(1, modulate, w). FH1 reads it at UV1 times a per-material scale (`c360_2`) that isn't decoded. The averaged-only version looked "remarkably flat" in-game. **Ground AO:** the `h_blnd*_ao` AO map (`AO_Sampler`, slot 5 on blnd2, 6 on blnd3) is the blend materials' `ambientOcclusionMap` on UV1, on every layer, and on plain terrain, near and far; not on vertex-blended ground, whose UV1 holds weight ramps. **Specular:** an object's specular map (`SpecularMapSampler`, slot 4) becomes its `roughnessMap`, 0.95 - 0.8 x brightness, 0.15 to 0.95 (`rough_texture`, `r<id>.data.dds`), by eye.

## Static LODs

- 250 m cells (`CELL`), one shape per cell and category.
- 3 details from FH1's LOD00, 01 and 02. If a group lacks a level, its coarsest mesh stands in.
- Switch distances: `LOD_DIST = (300, 800)`, from the shape's edge. Props vanish at `PROP_FAR = 1600`. `col` shapes never vanish.
- Detail sizes are computed from each shape's radius and `PX = 1250` (pixels of radius per radius/distance at 1440p), so all cells switch at the same distance. The engine measures from the centre, so `detail_sizes` adds the radius: tile groups can be 1 km wide. A name like `o0_a71` means detail size 71.
- Shape tree: `base00 > start01 > o<i>_a<size>`, as in stock shapes.
- A cell's meshes drawn at all three LODs go in a second shape, `c<u>_<cat>f`, with one detail (size `detail_sizes(...)[2]`) and its own `Colmesh`. In one shape they'd be cooked once per detail. `animated.py` reads ground from both `_col` and `_colf` shapes.
- Each LOD and `Colmesh` node is a full copy in the `.cdae`: size fits 56.1 bytes per instantiated vertex and 11.8 per triangle, R2 0.9999. `write_lod` welds vertices that would write the same text, which took Colorado's predicted cooked shapes from 2.80 GB to 1.91 GB. Three-detail shapes skip their middle level (`SKIPMID`, `FH1_MIDLOD=1` keeps it): LOD0 draws out to the LOD1 distance, 800 m, then LOD2, which drops one copy of most geometry for some fps. `Colmesh` is written as positions only and welded by position, which cut its vertices 38% on Red Rock. Leaving out normals and UVs doesn't shrink the cooked format itself: Red Rock's collision still cooked at about 56 bytes a vertex.

## Collision

`col` shapes (ground, roads, rock) collide with their LOD0 mesh, the one drawn up close. They used to collide with their coarsest LOD: roads felt bumpy, and a piece drawn only at LOD0 in a group with coarser copies had no collision at all. `pro` shapes (baked props) still collide with their coarsest LOD. World-mesh barriers, guardrails and fences (`armco|barrier|fence|rail`) go in `bar` shapes, which collide only with walls from `colfit.rails`. Each connected piece is halved along its long axis until a piece is at most 0.5 m thick and 8 m long, at least 30 cm thick. Pieces whose ends meet head-on within 1.5 m, across cell lines too, join into one wall swept along their centre line with mitred joints, carried by the cell of its first piece. Armco posts are their own pieces, reaching the ground about 0.5 m below the beam: those within 0.6 m of a wall are dropped and the wall goes down by their median drop. Separate boxes (the first try) caught the car at every joint and post. Clear Springs: 17,943 pieces, 161 walls, 61k triangles, for 615k LOD0 barrier triangles; 99.9% of drawn rail vertices lie within 36 cm of a wall face. Barriers and objects under 15 m have no LOD2 copy and vanish at 800 m (`FARCUT`). Each colliding shape has a `collision-1` node, one `Colmesh_0-1` holding all its colliding geometry (a `<triangles>` per material, so each keeps its `groundType`), and `collisionType: Collision Mesh` on the `TSStatic`. BeamNG's vehicle physics drops some collision meshes from a shape that has several: on 2026-11-03 `castRayStatic` (the vehicles' static collision) found nothing on 256 of 19,679 cell meshes, about 1.3%, while the scene raycast and the files had them. One was the road at (-2818, -926) in `c21029_col`'s `Colmesh_10`, which cars fell through. An exact copy of that shape lost the same mesh; the same pieces merged into one mesh collided. Material, mesh index, flatness, size and the shape's box don't predict which meshes drop. To check physics in-game: `castRayStatic(vec3(x, y, z + 1.5), vec3(0, 0, -1), 3)` from Lua, and `be:reloadStaticCollision(true)` after changing collision, which applies a frame or two later. `vis` shapes have none. Clear Springs world and prop collision after this change: 1.48M triangles. The user found road bumpiness and collision good in-game (2026-11-04).

Instanced props collide with simple shapes from `colfit.py` instead of their mesh, as each colliding object is its own copy in physics at about 41.5 bytes a triangle. Props named as fences, walls, gates, barriers, parked cars, poles, lights, signs, bins, benches, containers and the like, under 16 m across (`BOXED`), get one box in the model's frame, or two stacked boxes when that cuts the volume by 30% (barrier feet, a pole's crossarm). The rest get a convex hull of at most 24 points when they're under 8 m across and nearly convex (rocks, logs), or else a skyline of boxes (buildings, tents, pylons): on a grid over the footprint, each column runs from its lowest to its highest surface, and neighbouring columns of matching height merge, at most 40 boxes. A column's lowest surface can be high, so eaves, carport roofs and the lintel over a doorway float and can be driven under. A hull on a building sloped from the eaves to the ground, out in front of the walls, so buildings never get one. A shape takes the simple one whenever `colfit` makes one, even with more triangles, as boxes behave better in a crash (it used to need a 40% saving). Names a wheel may roll on (`KEEPMESH`: bridges, ramps, stages, decks, roads) always keep theirs, as seams between boxes would be bumps. Boxes are at least 10 cm thick, and everything is wound outward, written as one more `Colmesh` geometry that's never drawn (`write_lod(colmesh=...)`). Instanced collision was 8.0M triangles, about 317 MB of physics. A dry run on the source meshes gives 1.95M, about 77 MB: 167 shapes boxed, 194 skylines, 53 hulls, 383 left as meshes. `Skinned_Rope` (festival bunting) has no collision. `FH1_COLBOX=0` keeps the meshes. The user drove Red Rock with all three (2026-11-02) and found them good.

Every FH1 height gets `frame.LIFT` (150 m) added. BeamNG's physics puts a ground plane at z = 0 in a level with no `TerrainBlock`, and cars rode on it wherever FH1's ground dips below zero: the floating in Carson and on the golf course, and where the car landed after falling through the missing collision at (-2818, -926). FH1's drivable surfaces reach -46 m and the dam's gorge about -103 m. `frame.py` holds each level's centre and half size and `LIFT`; every script that turns FH1 positions into BeamNG ones adds it (`g2b` in `build3.py` and `nightlights.py`, and the record conversions in `animated.py`, `grass.py`, `particles.py`, `levelinfo.py` and `navmap.py`). Scripts that read the built shapes (`water.py`, `animated.py`'s ground) follow by themselves. `sky.py` raises `fogAtmosphereHeight` by the same amount.

Smashables have no collision, so the car drives through them as FH1's break on contact. `CollObjs.xml` places FH1's smashables, but its names match catalog meshes poorly (54 of 179 types). Its positions match exactly instead: a free-roam record within 5 cm of an entry (`SMASH`) is smashable. That's 3,831 records over 162 meshes, mostly snow poles, `FenceF`, signs, armco arrows, bins, wooden barriers, flower holders, picnic tables, benches and mailboxes. Their `TSStatic`s get `collisionType: None`, an instanced shape whose objects are all smashable is built without a collision node, and mirrored ones bake into `vis` cells. Not done: making them break or fly off, which would need jbeam props.

FH1's `.fiz` tiles were tried and reverted. Cars behaved as if under 10x gravity and crumpled, and RAM and fps got worse. The reader then had its offsets 0x20 bytes off, so triangles spiked to the world origin and looked mixed-wound; that's fixed (see `formats.md`). `.fiz` can't be the only collision: it holds roads and FH1's invisible road-edge walls, not terrain.

## Plants

Plants use BeamNG's forest system, not `TSStatic`. Items have `o0_a120`, `o*_a40` and `bb_autobillboard25`: LOD00, LOD01, then an imposter. `FH1_IMPOSTERS=0` keeps LOD01 at distance instead. Stride-16 tree cards are skipped by the world mesh path.

## Grass

`grass.py` (a post-step) places FH1's `Grass_Ungrouped` sets (see `formats.md`) as forest items. BeamNG's `GroundCover` needs a `TerrainBlock`, which the level doesn't have. One item per sprite would be 10.4M items, so each group of layers becomes clump shapes, `fh1_grass_<hash>_c<n>`: 4 variants of about 8 m2 (grown so sparse layers still get 3 sprites a clump), at FH1's density x area x weight. Sprites are crossed quads with FH1's sizes, heights x 1.3, normals up, alpha-tested and double-sided, casting no shadow (`fh1_grass_<tid>` in `art/forest/grass/main.materials.json`, textures `g<tid>` with colour bled into the cut-outs). Clumps lean with the ground up to 25 degrees, and density falls from 30 to 70 degrees of slope. Near a patch's outline, clumps become `_e<n>` ones of 0.75 m radius whose centres stay 0.75 m inside, so grass keeps off the asphalt. Two details: everything to 25 m, a third of the sprites to 45 m, then nothing. Corn: 120 and 240 m. The first in-game build (4 m2 clumps, shadows, 30/60 m, corn 150/300 m) cost enough fps to hover at 60, so these were cut; RAM was fine. No collision, no imposters. The seed is fixed, so builds repeat. The script removes its own items, shapes and `managedItemData` entries first, so it's safe to rerun, and reruns `grade.py` if that already ran.

Forest files list a matrix's axes as rows, like `TSStatic`s: tilted stock items on Italy, Utah and West Coast match their terrain normal by row, for example 288 against 40. `grass.py` transposes. `build3.py` still writes forest items untransposed, which only matters for tilted ones.

## People

**Festival crowd** (`grass.py`): FH1's 113 crowd sets (texture 20491, a 256 x 512 atlas of 6 rows of 8 people) become one forest item per sprite, `fh1_grass_crowd<n>`, one shape per sprite (48: 24 pairs 1 m wide, 24 singles 0.5 m). Each is one quad in the x-z plane, 1.65 m tall, scaled to FH1's 1.5 to 1.8 m (not by `GrassHeightScale`), in a node named `BBZ_o0_a<size>`: Torque's z-axis billboard, which turns the card to face the camera about up, as FH1 turns its sprites. The loader in the exe holds `BB::`, `BB_`, `BBZ::` and `BBZ_`; no stock shape uses them, so this is untested in-game. Density x area x weight per layer as FH1 (0.2 to 0.8 per m2): about 64k people. Drawn to 300 m. Their own seed (`CROWD_SEED`), so the grass doesn't move.

**Cutscene people** (`people.py`, a post-step): the characters FH1's cutscenes place in Colorado (`gamemodes.zip`, `Colorado/Cutscenes/cutscenes_default_freeroam.xml`, `cutscenes_press_start.xml`, `cutscenes_default_both.xml`: `CCutsceneAnimationTrigger` with `animname` Fest_*, Street_*, Driver_*, Nemesis_*, Barn_find_*...) held at frame 0. FH1 shows them only during their cutscene: the diner, street-race hub intros, wristband and skills unlocks, barn finds, title screens. 303 triggers, 170 spots, 70 models, about 1M triangles, one TSStatic per spot in `fh1_people` (the cutscene's name in `internalName`), no collision. Trigger x, y, z are in the `.pgeo` z sign and `rotation` is degrees about up, as `animated.py`'s `at()`. Skinned vertices blend their four bone weights at time 0 (`pose`); rigid meshes (heads, phones, cameras, cups) follow their bone. Meshes belong to Granny models in order, as many as each model's `MeshBindings`. Spots: a trigger is dropped within 1.5 m of one from another cutscene or a later event of the same one, or 3 m of the same model; people started together keep their places. Textures `art/textures/people_<name>.color.dds`, with an opacity map and alpha test when over 1/250 of texels are under half alpha (hair). Drawn to 200 m. Not yet seen in-game.

## Scene

`visibleDistance` 6000, or 24000 with the backdrop, `shadowDistance` 1600, `TimeOfDay` fixed, `ScatterSky`, one `SpawnSphere`. `build3.py`'s spawn is the first race route checkpoint near Red Rock, moved onto the road height, and `info.json` holds the title and spawn point. On the whole map `levelinfo.py` then lists the Horizon Festival and FH1's ten Horizon Outposts (`OUTPOST_<n>_NODE` in `GameObjs.xml`, placed on the node with its facing, 1 m up) instead, with Red Rock Outpost as the default. A chunk keeps `spawn_redrock` and gains the outposts inside it.

## Races

`races.py` writes one `timeTrial` mission per FH1 event to `gameplay/missions/<level>/timeTrial/`: `info.json` and `race.race.json` as stock time trials have them, plus a preview of the route on the map art. Events, names, routes and laps come from `gamedb.slt` and `EN.zip`'s string tables (`events.py`, `strtab.py`). The start trigger is the event's `<id>_NODE` in `GameObjs.xml`, FH1's event marker, or the pole position when there is none. Street, Nemesis and plane races use their checkpoints, with radius half the gate's width. Festival races have only GPS waypoints and finish cannons, as FH1 marked the course with barriers, so waypoints become 12 m checkpoints, cut where the route passes the finish trigger. A finish within 100 m of the pole makes a circuit with the event's laps. FH1 has no medal or target times for these races, which were against AI (`RacesSpecial`'s targets link to no Colorado race), so medal times are `Tracks.Length` times the laps over 28 m/s gold on asphalt or 20 m/s on dirt, with silver 1.15 and bronze 1.35 times gold. Not yet tried in-game.

The level starts at 9:30 (`TIME` in `sky.py`), as the user asked. The sky is a `SkyBox`, `fh1_skybox`, over the `ScatterSky`. `sky.py` bakes ten DXT1 cubemaps from `TimeOfDayA.xml`, at FH1's 0:00, 6:45, 7:25, 10:00, 12:00, 15:36, 18:10, 19:20, 19:55 and 20:40 (about 41 MB, 1024 per face). The 15:36 bake keeps the names `fh1_sky` and `fh1_sky_cubemap`, so the default look and the rivers' reflections are unchanged. Every bake scales the measured 15:36 gradient by FH1's atmosphere colours and `SkyGain` relative to 15:36, and uses FH1's cloud and ground colours for its own minute. Stars come from `Stars.bin`, faded by `StarFade`. In-game they were invisible as 0.6 px points, because mips averaged them away, so they're 0.9 px and bakes with `StarFade` over 0.1 build mips that keep the brightest of each 2x2 (`dds.save(peak=True)`). The moon is `Moon2.xds` (full moon with glow) at FH1's fixed night position, about 11 degrees up in the south-east, faded by `SunObjectMoon` and `SunObjectAlpha`, and 3 degrees wide by eye. FH1's sun never sets: its night runs 20:02 to 6:28. BeamNG's legacy sun sets at 18:00, so each bake takes over at a BeamNG hour chosen by phase: night 20:30, predawn 5:00, sunrise 5:45, morning 6:45, midday 8:30, afternoon 14:00, sunset 16:45, golden 17:30, dusk 18:00, twilight 19:00. `fh1_sky.json` lists the bakes and the bake for each quarter hour. Every 2 s, `fh1Extras.lua` checks it, and when the bake changes it sets the skybox `material`, `theLevelInfo.globalEnviromentMap` and every `River`'s `cubemap`, each followed by `postApply`. Switches are cuts, as a `SkyBox` can't cross-fade. Bakes in `BEAMNG_SKY` (night, 20:30 to 5:00) set `hideBox`, and the Lua hides the skybox so BeamNG's `ScatterSky` and `core_celestial` draw the night: their stars are additive and stay visible at full night, where the baked ones faded with the scene. The user chose this over FH1's stars. The night bake still feeds reflections. In-game the switching works. The user found FH1's 9:00 bake, held until 10:30, far yellower than FH1, which is blue most of the day: FH1's horizon is only yellow from about 8:00 to 9:45, so morning is now 10:00 and short. The user found the sky, sunset and night good in-game (2026-11-04).

Fog and bloom follow FH1's post-processing zones and time of day. `PostProcessingZones_Safe.xml` gives 16 zones as triangles, each naming a fog and a bloom template from `dynamicpost.zip` and a fade of 15 to 30 s. Fog templates differ only in density: `NoFog` and `LightFog` 1.0, `Redrock` 2.35, `Mountains` 3.58. Bloom templates set cutoff (1 or 1.5) and scale (0.9 to 1.2). Colour settings are the same in every zone, so their only difference is the colour grade LUT, which BeamNG 0.40 can't change at runtime. `sky.py` writes the zones into `fh1_sky.json` with fog relative to `Redrock` (`FOG_REF`), since the level's fog was tuned at Red Rock, so most of the map gets 0.43 times it and Finley Dam 1.52. Each bake also carries FH1's `FogDensity` relative to 15:36: about 2.7 times at night. Every 0.5 s `fh1Extras.lua` finds the zone under the camera, holding the last one in the gaps between zones, and eases fog and bloom towards it over the zone's fade time. Fog is `theLevelInfo.fogDensity` with `postApply`, only when it moves 0.5%. Bloom is the global `PostEffectBloomObject`: `threshHold` (engine default 8) times the cutoff and `intensity` times the scale, set every update because a graphics settings change resets them. Bloom is put back when the level ends, so other levels keep the engine's. Checked in LuaJIT with stubbed objects, not in-game. In 0.40 the fog colour comes from the sun and sky lighting: `fogColor` reaches only particles and `ScatterSky.fogScale` probably does nothing, so FH1's `FogColour` curves aren't used. Its tonemapper, exposure range and saturation have no runtime field either.

## Rotation lists

A `TSStatic`'s `rotationMatrix` lists its axes as rows: stock guardrail chains match rows 408 to 43 across 12 levels. `build3.py` works with axes as columns, so it transposes on writing. Forest items list them as rows too (see Grass), but `build3.py` writes them untransposed.

## Always-on ground

Any world mesh whose zone record has a first LOD distance of 1e5 or more (`SHELL`) is coarse ground, whatever its name. This covers `Mountains_Area04A_Terrain_05`, which sits over the Finley Dam area and shares a `lodkey` with the fine tile `Terrain_LOD00_05`. No hex near the dam loads it, but the hex ground fallback (cells no hex draws ground for) filled the hole the dam stands in with it, so its 100 m triangles hung over the dam's face as a tan dome. Since 2026-11-04 the fallback skips cells under large placed structures (`STRUCTS`, the footprints `structures()` rasters for the far tiers). Clear Springs has none of those cells.

World-space objects (not ground) go through the zones too: `zone_objects` keeps a mesh only where one of the 8 hexes nearest some point of its box loads its LOD ring. Colorado audit (2026-11-04): 5,886 of 6,062 objects pass over their whole box, 6 over most of it, and 170 nowhere. Those 170 are 58 power line spans no zone loads at all (817 others are loaded), about 100 catalog meshes whose only record is at the origin (drawn 22 m underground at FH1's 0, 0) and spare LODs such as `Barrier_020_Armco_LOD01_019`. Clear Springs drops 111 meshes, 10k triangles. The hex fallback still fills cells no hex loads ground for; the log line `hex fallback by ring` lists what fills them. In Clear Springs that's all 1,545 cells, mostly `TERR_CLRD_FEST_16_LOD01` (561) and `Reservoir_Area2_MID_06_NOLOD` (424), none loaded by a near hex.

Far ground is hidden over water, and only sinks under near ground, not at road edges.

## Cooked shapes

Stock levels ship a `.cdae` beside every `.dae`. `cookship.py` copies BeamNG's cooked shapes from `temp/` into the level, after one load. A rebuild wipes them. First run on the whole map on 2026-11-02: 6,481 of 6,761 shapes, 1.5 GB. The 280 left are `wat` shapes, which `water.py` replaces with Rivers so they never load, and 3 instanced shapes that weren't drawn during the load. Not yet confirmed in-game that the next load skips the import.

The Uber shell has festival tents baked in as lumps. In a small hole in finer ground (under 0.15 km²), shell vertices are held to 1 m above that ground (`lump`).
